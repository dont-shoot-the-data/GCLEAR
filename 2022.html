Homicides of App-Based Gig Drivers (Uber, Lyft, DoorDash, Delivery)

Combined analysis and case-by-case dataset of every documented 2022 homicide of an app-based gig driver in the United States. Master victim list: Gig Workers Rising's report Murdered Behind the Wheel: An Escalating Crisis for App Drivers (with ACRE and PowerSwitch Action) and the GWR-compiled public database of app workers killed on the job, verified by The Markup. Every case below was then independently re-verified against local news, prosecutors' offices, and court records, with statuses current through July 2026.
Gig Workers Rising's published count for 2022 (at least 31 app-based workers murdered while working) matches the 31 cases in this dataset. The Corrections to Circulating Lists section at the bottom of this page, after the dataset, addresses claims from viral social media compilations that circulated partial versions of this list; those compilations are not cited as a source for any fact.

Corrections to Circulating Lists

Versions of this victim list circulate publicly, in Gig Workers Rising's database and in viral social media compilations that began spreading in 2023. Every case on this page was verified independently against news reports, prosecutors' offices, and court records; this section corrects the errors in those circulating versions, and the compilations themselves are not a source for any fact in this dataset.
UndercountClaim: "In 2022, 13 Lyft/Uber drivers were murdered while driving."
At least 19 Uber or Lyft rideshare drivers were murdered on the job in 2022, and at least 31 drivers across all app-based platforms, matching Gig Workers Rising's published 2022 count. The compilations missed 18 of the 31 cases, including every delivery-platform case except one, and miscounted Kon Fung, a food delivery driver, as an Uber rideshare driver.
Partially SupportedClaim: "Every single perpetrator is either black (9) or unknown likely black (4)."
Within the 13 rideshare cases the compilations covered, the documented record does show that all charged individuals with a publicly documented race are Black. Across the full 31-case 2022 universe the claim fails: of 27 murder-charged individuals, 16 have a documented race, and 3 of those 16 are not Black (Aaron Kelly, White per law enforcement description, in the Krumbah case; Mario Esparza and Anderson Martinez-Herrera, Hispanic, in the Satavu and Jenkins-Perry cases). The other 11 charged individuals have no publicly documented race, and 10 cases remain unsolved. "Unknown, likely black" is an inference, not documentation; this dataset codes unknowns as unknown.
Errors CorrectedFactual errors in the compilations:
Richard Skelskey was killed on a Lyft trip in Memphis, Tennessee (listed as Uber, "Memphis, IL"). Yolanda Dillion was killed December 8, not December 9. James Barron was killed November 28, not November 29. Caron Arterberry was 36, not 37. Manuel Francis-Hernandez was shot March 19 and died March 23. Da'Jour Russ, listed without an age, was 22.
Status UpdatesCompilation case statuses are frozen at October 2023.
Since then, twelve cases in this dataset reached final adjudications: Crew, Mitchell and Peaks, Powell, Phillips, Williams, Gossett, Esparza, Martinez-Herrera, Simmons, and Shannon were convicted or pled guilty, and the Jacobs and Kelly cases closed with not-guilty-by-reason-of-insanity verdicts. Four suspects were also charged in the previously unsolved Mohamed case, and a juvenile was charged in the Childers case.
Methodology: The master victim list is Gig Workers Rising's report Murdered Behind the Wheel: An Escalating Crisis for App Drivers (with ACRE and PowerSwitch Action) and the GWR-compiled public database of app workers killed on the job, verified by The Markup; GWR's 2022 count of at least 31 matches this dataset. Every case was then independently verified against local news, prosecutors' offices, and court records. Claims examined in the corrections section above come from viral social media compilations; those compilations are not cited as a source for any fact. Inclusion criteria: homicide of a driver working for an app-based rideshare or delivery platform at the time of the killing, or killed in direct connection with that work; traffic accidents excluded. Inclusion is motive-blind, matching GWR's murdered-while-working standard: drivers killed on the job for reasons unconnected to the work (the Raymond and Krumbah cases) are counted, with circumstances stated in the summaries. Police-involved killings of drivers would be included and flagged separately; none occurred in 2022 (the officer-involved shooting connected to the Allen case killed the suspect, not the driver, and the case is counted normally). Suspect race comes from a booking, prison or court record, a published photo, or a news report, and the Race source column shows which; where the author judged race from a photo without a record it reads “Author call,” surnames are never treated as documentation, and anyone whose race could not be verified is left unverified. The Relationship column codes the victim-suspect connection per case: passenger where trip records, app data, or in-ride circumstances document the suspect as the rider (The Markup's verified carjacking dataset supplies the app-summons coding in the Khan and Jenkins-Perry cases); stranger where reporting documents the encounter as a robbery, carjacking, random attack, road-rage encounter, or a third party to someone else's dispute, with no prior connection reported; prior relationships only as stated in reporting; not documented where a suspect is identified but the encounter itself is unestablished; and unknown for unsolved cases. The Saffolds entry is carried from the GWR dataset, which sources it to a Chicago Tribune overnight-violence roundup and a police report; no independent press coverage was located, and his age, race, and cause of death are coded as not verified. Where GWR lists found or report dates, this dataset uses verified incident dates (Spicuzza killed Feb 10, found Feb 12; Garland killed Jun 30, not Jul 5; Dillion killed Dec 8, not Dec 9; McCormack killed overnight into Jan 24; Price killed Nov 2, announced Nov 4; Hill shot Jun 26, died Jun 28). "Solved/Charged" counts any case with at least one murder-charged suspect or a closed adjudication; juvenile defendants in the McCormack, Khan, Fung, and Childers cases have no publicly reported adjudication outcomes, reflecting juvenile-record confidentiality. Fung's platform is coded as a food delivery app due to conflicting outlet reporting. All dates, ages, sentences, and statuses verified as of July 2026.
