Corrections to Circulating Lists

Versions of this victim list circulate publicly in viral social media compilations; no institutional list exists for 2024. Every case on this page was verified independently against news reports, police statements, prosecutors' offices, and court records. This section corrects the errors in those circulating versions, and the compilations themselves are not a source for any fact in this dataset.
Errors CorrectedYear and date errors in the circulating lists:
Circulating lists carry the Sherali killing twice, under both May 17, 2024 and May 17, 2026; Fort Worth police and Tarrant County jail records place it on May 17, 2024, and the 2026 entry is a duplicate. Lists date the Kubay killing January 25, the morning his body was found; residents heard the shots the night of January 24, when deputies believe he was killed. Some reports date the Afzal shooting June 21; the NYPD places it at 10 PM on June 20. One list places the Folks killing in Atlanta; it occurred in Union City, in south Fulton County.
Errors CorrectedPlatform codings corrected:
Lists code Renteria as a Lyft driver; local reporting identifies Uber. Lists code Afzal as an Uber driver; the press identifies only a rideshare, and his platform is coded unverified here. Lists code Linares as Uber Eats; most outlets report only that he was delivering a Walmart grocery order and one outlet reported Uber, so his platform is carried from the lists with that conflict noted.
Partially SupportedCompilations code at-large, unnamed, and juvenile suspects as Black by default. This dataset codes suspect race only where it can be verified. Of the 20 counted suspects in 2024, 17 have a verified race: 12 Black, 2 White, 2 Hispanic, 1 Other. The other 3 are left unverified rather than assigned a race.
Status UpdatesCompilation case statuses are frozen at compile time.
Since the circulating lists were compiled: Waggoner was convicted and sentenced to 235 months (September 2025); Baggott was convicted of first-degree murder (July 2025) and sentenced to life without release (March 2026); Hamid was convicted (June 2026) and sentenced to 40 years to life (July 2026); Brock was convicted (January 2026) and sentenced to 21 years to life (February 2026); Minor pleaded guilty to voluntary manslaughter and received 29 years (October 2025); and all four adult-charged defendants in the Jama case pleaded no contest and were sentenced by March 2026, while charges against the 11-year-old were dropped on competency grounds. Arrests the lists do not reflect: a 15-year-old charged in the Leiva case (April 2025), a juvenile charged in the Kelvin case (April 2026), and two further arrests in the Badee case (January 2025).
Methodology: No institutional victim list exists for 2024: Gig Workers Rising's Murdered Behind the Wheel report and public database, the anchor for the earlier years of this project, end in April 2023. This dataset was compiled directly from local news reports, police statements, prosecutors' offices, and court records, and its totals should be read as floors. Claims examined in the corrections section above come from viral social media compilations; those compilations are not cited as a source for any fact. Inclusion criteria: homicide of a driver working for an app-based rideshare or delivery platform (Amazon Flex included) at the time of the killing, or killed in direct connection with that work. Killings while working but not because of the job are included motive-blind with circumstances stated, matching the institutional list's murdered-while-working standard; the Renteria case (crossfire aimed at his passenger) is included on that basis, and the Jama case, in which the driver was ambushed at his home moments after completing his final trip, is included under the same standard, which counts drivers killed immediately after their last trip. Non-criminal traffic accidents are excluded. No police-involved killing of a driver was documented in 2024; the Folks case, in which the killer was an off-duty officer riding as an ordinary Lyft passenger, is counted as a regular homicide. Bias-motive coding is omitted for this dataset. Suspect race comes from a booking, prison or court record, a published photo, or a news report, and the Race source column shows which; where the author judged race from a photo without a record it reads “Author call,” surnames are never treated as documentation, and anyone whose race could not be verified is left unverified. Relationship between killer and victim is coded documented-only: Passenger (rode on the fatal trip), Customer (delivery recipient), Stranger with the mechanism noted, Known dispute, or Ex-partner where applicable; where a suspect is charged but the relationship is unreported it is coded Not documented, and unsolved cases always code Unknown regardless of any suspected mechanism. "Solved/Charged" counts any case with at least one murder-charged or murder-arrested suspect. The Wilmot case carries its own status: police identified the shooter and submitted the case to the Clark County District Attorney for a self-defense review, no public charging decision was found, and it is counted in neither the solved/charged nor the unsolved figures. Juvenile and withheld-name defendants in the Jama, Kelvin, Leiva, and Badee matters reflect identity-confidentiality practices; the Kelvin suspect's name appears because Oregon press published it. All dates, ages, sentences, and statuses verified as of August 2026.
