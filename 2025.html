Corrections to Circulating Lists

Versions of this victim list circulate publicly in viral social media compilations; no institutional list exists for 2025. Every case on this page was verified independently against news reports, police statements, prosecutors' offices, and court records. This section corrects the errors in those circulating versions, and the compilations themselves are not a source for any fact in this dataset.
Errors CorrectedOne case removed:
Circulating lists include a January 10 killing in Brodnax, Virginia, of an unnamed rideshare driver in his 40s, for which Zakaila Stith and Isabelle Battle were charged with aggravated murder. Investigators stated the driver was not working through Uber, Lyft, or any app platform on the fatal ride, which was a private arrangement. The case falls outside this dataset's inclusion standard and is excluded from the table and every total on this page.
Errors CorrectedOne case added:
No circulating list carries Aaron Giles, the GoPuff driver and father of nine shot by armed men who had cased a Tallahassee apartment complex before he arrived with a delivery on March 23. One suspect was charged within three weeks. His case appears as row 8.
Errors CorrectedPlatform codings corrected:
Lists code the Adetula ride as Uber; charging documents identify a Lyft booking. Lists code the Webster platform as Veo, a scooter company; he drove for Veyo, an app-based medical-transport service. Lists code Ramey as DoorDash per his family, while one official-sourced report said Grubhub; the family coding is carried with that conflict noted. Lists code Kiprono as Uber, matching contemporaneous police statements, but the Shreveport Times later corrected its coverage to Lyft; the conflict is noted in his row. One outlet coded Jackson as Uber Eats; police described a rideshare pickup and Uber confirmed he was working, so Uber is carried. Whether the fatal Lewis ride was booked through the Lyft app was still undetermined in available reporting.
Errors CorrectedDate, place, and name errors:
Lists date the Bolaji killing April 18; police place the shooting at about 2 AM on April 16. Lists date the Jackson case January 29, the day his body was found; he vanished on a January 26 pickup. Reports variously dated the Kiprono shooting November 16 and November 21; the indictment dates it November 19. Lists place the Gbedee killing in Durham, his home city; he was killed in Johnston County near Benson. Lists place the Lucas killing in Tempe, where the ride began; he was killed in Guadalupe. Lists place the Campbell killing in Colorado Springs; the killing site is unincorporated El Paso County. Lists place the Nguyen killing in Houston; it occurred in unincorporated northwest Harris County. Lists carry the Jackson suspect as "Iquane Brow"; the charged name is Iquane Brown. Lists show Lyft drivers killed in Charlotte on October 23 in both 2024 (Leiva) and 2025 (Grant); the identical dates are a coincidence, not a duplicate.
Partially SupportedCompilations code at-large, unnamed, juvenile, and group suspects as Black by default. This dataset codes suspect race only where it can be verified. Of the 23 counted suspects in 2025, 20 have a verified race: 16 Black, 3 Hispanic, 1 White. The other 3 are left unverified rather than assigned a race.
Status UpdatesCompilation case statuses are frozen at compile time.
Since the circulating lists were compiled: Simms was convicted of second-degree murder and sentenced to 37.5 to 80 years (January 2026); grand juries indicted Joseph and Martinez in the Stemley case (January 2026) and Thomas, Loston, and Scott in the Kiprono case (February 2026); the Kim suspect the lists carry as unnamed was identified and charged with capital murder (Perkins); federal prosecutors took over the Andrus case with a capital carjacking-resulting-in-death indictment (September 2025); the Grant case closed when police killed Andrade and prosecutors cleared the officers (May 2026); Ethan Bush died fleeing arrest in the Lewis case, leaving his brother charged alone; and the Dawson trial in the Campbell case was set for April 13, 2026, with no public verdict found.
Methodology: No institutional victim list exists for 2025: Gig Workers Rising's Murdered Behind the Wheel report and public database, the anchor for the earlier years of this project, end in April 2023. This dataset was compiled directly from local news reports, police statements, prosecutors' offices, and court records, and its totals should be read as floors. Claims examined in the corrections section above come from viral social media compilations; those compilations are not cited as a source for any fact. Inclusion criteria: homicide of a driver working for an app-based rideshare or delivery platform (Amazon Flex and Veyo included) at the time of the killing, or killed in direct connection with that work. Killings while working but not because of the job are included motive-blind with circumstances stated, matching the institutional list's murdered-while-working standard: the Bolaji and Kiprono cases (ambushes aimed at their passengers), and the Grant case (an attempted-carjacking spree) are included on that basis. Vehicle deaths are excluded even when a driver was charged, because this study counts intentional killings only: the Habib and Sidiya cases (fleeing motorists) and the Snell and Diakite cases (crashes) are excluded on that basis. The Webster and Stemley killings stay in because the vehicle was used deliberately as the weapon. A January 10 Brodnax, Virginia killing carried on circulating lists is excluded because investigators stated the fatal ride was arranged off-app. No police-involved killing of a driver was documented in 2025; in the Grant case police killed the perpetrator, not the driver. Bias-motive coding is omitted for this dataset. Suspect race comes from a booking, prison or court record, a published photo, or a news report, and the Race source column shows which; where the author judged race from a photo without a record it reads “Author call,” surnames are never treated as documentation, and anyone whose race could not be verified is left unverified. Group codings on circulating lists do not attach to later-identified individuals and revert to not documented. Relationship between killer and victim is coded documented-only: Passenger (rode on or booked the fatal trip), Customer (delivery), Stranger with the mechanism noted, Known dispute, or Ex-partner where applicable; where a suspect is charged but the relationship is unreported it is coded Not documented, and unsolved cases always code Unknown regardless of any suspected mechanism. "Solved/Charged" counts any case with at least one murder-charged or murder-arrested suspect. The Grant case carries its own Closed status: the killer was identified by police and is deceased, so no prosecution is possible, and it is counted in neither the solved/charged nor the unsolved figures. Juvenile and withheld-name defendants in the Nguyen and Kim matters reflect identity-confidentiality practices; the Gomez name appears because he was charged as an adult and named by authorities and the press. All dates, ages, sentences, and statuses verified as of August 2026.
