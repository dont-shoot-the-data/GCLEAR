Corrections to Circulating Lists

Versions of this victim list circulate publicly, in Gig Workers Rising's database and in viral social media compilations. Every case on this page was verified independently against news reports, police statements, prosecutors' offices, and court records. This section corrects the errors in those circulating versions, and the compilations themselves are not a source for any fact in this dataset.
Roster CorrectedRossana Delgado is carried on circulating lists as a 2021 Lyft-driver victim; the record does not support it.
Lyft told The Markup her killing did not occur on its platform, and the Georgia Bureau of Investigation told The Markup its investigation revealed no evidence she worked for Lyft. Contemporaneous reporting identifies Delgado, 37, as an Atlanta-area taxi driver who was lured to a DeKalb County mall by a drug-trafficking organization under the pretense of a shopping trip on April 16, 2021, held for days, and killed at a Cherry Log cabin. Her killing was real and is prosecuted: ten defendants were sentenced, including ringleader Edwin Murillo, who received life in September 2023, with three still at large at last report. It was not an app-based-driver homicide, and she is excluded from this dataset under its inclusion criteria. Two other names Lyft disputed on the same list, Glynon Nelson and Terrell Harris, are retained: a court affidavit states Nelson was driving for Lyft that night on another driver's account, and Harris is carried with the platform dispute noted on his row.

Status UpdatesCompilation case statuses are frozen at compile time.
Since the circulating lists were compiled: Skipper was convicted of second-degree murder and sentenced to 17 to 40 years, affirmed on appeal in June 2026; Marshall pleaded guilty and received 45 years (March 2023); Alissa was convicted on all counts and received 10 consecutive life terms (September 2024); Young was convicted at trial and received 41 years to life (April 2023); Wilson pleaded guilty and received 25 years (October 2023); Powe pleaded guilty and received life with parole after 19 years (May 2024); Baker pleaded no contest to voluntary manslaughter for 31 years 4 months (March 2024); Jarrett pleaded guilty and received 35 years (April 2024); Stokes was convicted of first-degree murder at trial (February 2023); and both teens in the Perkins case were sentenced in May 2022. Arrests the lists do not reflect: Domena was charged with murdering Singh in December 2021, and McKoy and Winmon were charged with murdering Metzger in July 2022. Methodology: Victim list anchored to Gig Workers Rising's Murdered Behind the Wheel report and public database, the institutional victim list for this era, then independently verified case by case; every fact on this page cites local news, police statements, prosecutors' offices, or court records, and the corrections section above documents where the circulating lists are wrong. Claims examined there also come from viral social media compilations; those compilations are not cited as a source for any fact. Inclusion criteria: homicide of a driver working for an app-based rideshare or delivery platform (Amazon Flex included) at the time of the killing, or killed in direct connection with that work. Killings while working but not because of the job are included motive-blind with circumstances stated, matching the institutional list's murdered-while-working standard: the Murray case (mass shooting while shopping an order), the Riley case (random gunfire while delivering), the Singh case (stray bullet while carrying passengers), and the Berry case (crossfire aimed at his passenger) are included on that basis. The murdered-while-working standard also counts drivers killed immediately after their final trip: the Bablu case (killed for his delivery e-bike minutes after a long shift) and the Yusufi case (killed resting in his car between overnight shifts on a trip made solely to drive) are included under it. The Delgado case, carried on circulating lists, is excluded because the investigating agency and the platform both found no evidence of app-based work; it is documented in the corrections section only. Non-criminal traffic accidents are excluded. No police-involved killing of a driver was documented in 2021; in the Zhu and Lewis cases police killed the perpetrator after the murder, and those cases carry the Closed status: killer identified and deceased, no prosecution possible. Bias-motive coding is omitted for this dataset. Suspect race comes from a booking, prison or court record, a published photo, or a news report, and the Race source column shows which; where the author judged race from a photo without a record it reads “Author call,” surnames are never treated as documentation, and anyone whose race could not be verified is left unverified. Relationship between killer and victim is coded documented-only: Passenger (summoned the driver through the app or rode on the fatal trip), Customer (delivery recipient), Stranger with the mechanism noted, Known dispute, or Ex-partner where applicable; where a suspect is charged but the relationship is unreported it is coded Not documented, and unsolved cases always code Unknown regardless of any suspected mechanism. "Solved/Charged" counts any case with at least one murder-charged or murder-arrested suspect. Juvenile and withheld-name defendants in the Anwar, Wehber, and Schelstraete matters reflect identity-confidentiality practices. All dates, ages, sentences, and statuses verified as of August 2026.
