Chart 7: Relationship of Killer to Victim (N = 27 homicides)
Documented relationship between the killer and the driver, across the 27 homicides. Unsolved cases always code Unknown by rule; the police-involved case is excluded.
Finding: In 8 of the 20 solved or charged homicides (40.0%), the killer reached the driver through the platform itself: 7 were passengers on the fatal trip (Levin, Bardales Valle, Diaz, Orozco, Torres, Piedra Garcia, Al Hijoj) and 1 was the delivery recipient (Cooke). Counting the Arocha-Duque ambush, where the fatal pickup was booked on a stolen rideshare account, the app was the access vector in 9 of 20 (45.0%). Ten homicides were committed by strangers: two carjackings (Ahmad Yar, Geda), two ambushes targeting the driver's passenger (Stiller, Grund), one gang drive-by that killed the driver as a bystander (Djembissi), one fleeing-driver collision (Kamara), two random street shootings (Kabba, Hagos), one ambush after a same-night altercation (Greer), and the lured Arocha-Duque ambush. One killing arose from a documented ongoing dispute (Snyder), and in one charged case the relationship is not publicly documented (Nims). Hard rule: unsolved cases always code Unknown, whatever the suspected mechanism. The coding scheme also includes Ex-partner; 2023 has no such case.

Corrections to Circulating Lists

Versions of this victim list circulate publicly, in Gig Workers Rising's database and in viral social media compilations. Every case on this page was verified independently against news reports, prosecutors' offices, and court records; this section corrects the errors in those circulating versions.
Errors CorrectedDate errors in the circulating lists:
The Gig Workers Rising database dates the Bardales Valle killing January 8; New Orleans police records place it March 8. Viral social media compilations date the Diaz killing March 16 (the arrest warrant says March 9), the Cooke killing April 24 (he was killed April 19), the Cole disappearance May 11 (he vanished mid-shift April 28), and the Kamara crash March 14 (it occurred March 15). One working list dated the Grund killing October 19; the Jacksonville Sheriff's Office places it October 6.
FalseClaim: Johnell Hampton was the suspect in the Stiller case.
A viral compilation lists Hampton as the suspect. Hampton, 21, was Andrew Stiller's rideshare passenger, the apparent target of the ambush, and was murdered alongside him. The shooter convicted at an April 2025 trial is Tyree Quinn.
Errors CorrectedRace and platform codings corrected:
Compilations code Phoebe Copas (the Piedra Garcia suspect) as Hispanic; she is White per extensive published photo and court coverage. Compilations code Adriana Arocha-Duque as White; she was a Venezuelan asylum seeker. Milton Pillacela Ayora is widely listed as an Uber driver; Lyft's own statement and Chicago reporting identify the fatal trip as a Lyft ride, though a police report obtained by Gig Workers Rising listed Uber.
Partially SupportedCompilations code at-large, unnamed, and juvenile suspects as Black by default.
This dataset codes suspect race only where publicly documented. Of the 30 murder-charged individuals in 2023, 14 have no publicly documented race, including the withheld-name Orozco arrestee, the juvenile who pleaded guilty in the Ahmad Yar case, and all five defendants indicted in the Djembissi killing. Among the 16 coded suspects, 14 are Black (1 documented in court records, 13 provisional pending photo verification), 1 is White (Copas, documented), and 1 is Hispanic (Solis, provisional). As in 2022, the record does not support coding every unknown as Black, and 2023 adds documented non-Black charged suspects.
Methodology: Victim list compiled from Gig Workers Rising's Murdered Behind the Wheel report and public database (which end in April 2023), supplemented by independent discovery sweeps for May through December 2023, then verified case by case against local news, prosecutors' offices, and court records; the lead lists are not cited as a source for any fact and their errors are corrected above. Inclusion criteria: homicide of a driver working for an app-based rideshare or delivery platform (Amazon Flex included) at the time of the killing, or killed in direct connection with that work. Killings while working but not because of the job (stray bullets, random attacks, crossfire) are included motive-blind with circumstances stated, matching the institutional list's murdered-while-working standard. Non-criminal traffic accidents are excluded; one vehicular homicide (Kamara) is included because the driver was criminally charged with murder. The police-involved killing (Henderson) is counted in victim totals and broken out of homicide and suspect statistics. Bias-motive coding is omitted for this dataset. Suspect race is recorded only where publicly documented via booking photo, court imagery, or law enforcement description; unknowns are coded as unknown, never inferred. Relationship between killer and victim is coded documented-only: Passenger (rode on the fatal trip), Customer (delivery recipient), Stranger with the mechanism noted, Known dispute, or Ex-partner where applicable; where a suspect is charged but the relationship is unreported it is coded Not documented, and unsolved cases always code Unknown regardless of any suspected mechanism. Codings marked † are provisional, resting on lead-list identification plus published booking or court imagery, and await a final photo verification pass; the same applies to victim codings drawn from lead-list identification, and one victim coding (McMillon) remains pending. "Solved/Charged" counts any case with at least one murder-charged or murder-arrested suspect; the Piedra Garcia case is counted there and closed, as the charged suspect died before trial. Juvenile and withheld-name defendants in the Orozco, Ahmad Yar, and Djembissi-adjacent matters reflect identity-confidentiality practices. All dates, ages, sentences, and statuses verified as of July 2026.
