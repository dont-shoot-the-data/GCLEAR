Corrections to Circulating Lists

Versions of this victim list circulate publicly, in Gig Workers Rising's database and in viral social media compilations. Every case on this page was verified independently against news reports, police statements, prosecutors' offices, and court records. This section corrects the errors in those circulating versions, and the compilations themselves are not a source for any fact in this dataset.
Errors CorrectedThe circulating 2017 roster carries one death that was not a homicide.
Gig Workers Rising's database counts four 2017 deaths; one of them, Manhattan driver Randolph Tolk, is excluded here. Tolk, 68, was struck on the head with a hockey stick in a road-rage altercation the night of Nov 11, 2017 and crashed blocks later, but the Manhattan District Attorney dropped the manslaughter charge against the attacker in June 2018 after the Medical Examiner concluded Tolk died of an underlying medical condition, not the attack. With no homicide ruling standing, the death falls outside this dataset's inclusion criteria and is documented here only. Lists also date the incident Nov 12; the attack and crash were the night of Nov 11.
Errors CorrectedYear and date errors in the circulating lists:
Circulating lists carry the Foster killing under 2018; contemporaneous Cleveland coverage, the wire report, and the calendar place it shortly after midnight on Monday, Aug 28, 2017. The institutional list dates the Nelson killing Sep 30 and places it in Skokie; it occurred May 30 in Lincolnwood, four months earlier, and some outlets misprint the date as May 20. The institutional list dates the Medina-Chevez killing May 1; he left home to pick up passengers the night of May 20 and his body was found May 25.
Errors CorrectedA 2017 killing is missing from every circulating list.
The May 24 Miami case, in which a federal jury convicted Jorge Aponte Figueroa of carjacking and murdering an Uber driver who was sleeping in his minivan, appears on no circulating list. The victim's name was never publicly released; the case is documented through federal prosecutors and court records.
Partially SupportedCompilations code at-large, unnamed, and juvenile suspects as Black by default.
This dataset codes suspect race only where publicly documented. Of the 5 murder-charged individuals in 2017, 4 have no publicly documented race (Moran, Adams, Stevens, Aponte Figueroa), and the year's only documented charged killer is White: Wasni, coded through the description the dying driver gave police and extensive published court imagery. The acquitted defendant in the Mecklenburg case is excluded from suspect statistics entirely. As on every other year page, the record does not support coding every unknown as Black.
Status UpdatesCompilation case statuses are frozen at compile time.
Since the circulating lists were compiled: Wasni pleaded guilty and was sentenced to 27 years (August 2020); Adams was convicted at trial and sentenced to life without parole (August 2021), after Stevens pleaded guilty and received 25 years (2018); Aponte Figueroa was convicted by a federal jury (March 2022) and sentenced to life (February 2023); and Johnson, the only person ever charged in the Mecklenburg killing, was acquitted at trial (November 2022), leaving that case legally unresolved.
Methodology: The victim list for 2017 is anchored to Gig Workers Rising's Murdered Behind the Wheel report and public database, the only institutional list of app-based drivers murdered on the job, which covers 2017 through April 2023; every case was then independently verified and the roster expanded against local news reports, police statements, prosecutors' offices, and court records. Inclusion criteria: homicide of a driver working for an app-based rideshare or delivery platform at the time of the killing, or killed in direct connection with that work. Killings while working but not because of the job are included motive-blind with circumstances stated, matching the institutional list's murdered-while-working standard; the Miami case, in which the driver was attacked while sleeping in his work vehicle, is included under that standard, which counts drivers killed resting between trips. Criminal vehicular homicides with a murder-charged driver are included (the Parker-Iverson case); non-criminal traffic accidents are excluded. Deaths with no standing homicide ruling are excluded: the Tolk case, carried on circulating lists, is documented in the corrections section only because the Medical Examiner attributed the death to an underlying medical condition and the charge was dropped. No police-involved killing of a driver was documented in 2017. Bias-motive coding is omitted for this dataset. Suspect race is recorded only where publicly documented via booking photo, court imagery, or law enforcement description; unknowns are coded as unknown, never inferred, and neither surnames nor complexion descriptions are treated as documentation. Because 2017 predates the compiled lists used on later pages, codings are textual or documented-description only and no provisional photo-based codings are carried. Relationship between killer and victim is coded documented-only: Passenger (rode on the fatal trip), Customer (delivery), Stranger with the mechanism noted, Known dispute, or Ex-partner where applicable; unsolved cases always code Unknown regardless of any suspected mechanism, and the Mecklenburg case also codes Unknown because the acquittal leaves the killer legally undetermined. "Solved/Charged" counts any case with at least one murder-charged suspect whose charge ended in conviction or remains pending. The Mecklenburg case carries its own status: the only person ever charged was acquitted at trial in 2022, and the case is counted in neither the solved/charged nor the unsolved figures; the acquitted defendant is excluded from all suspect statistics. All dates, ages, sentences, and statuses verified as of August 2026.
